# Learning safety

Separate observed evidence from inferred interpretations. Describe evidence quality and limitations. Telemetry alone must not establish a legal violation, legal compliance or an unsupported claim of certainty. Avoid diagnostic or punitive labels.

Explain learner-facing scores with contributing evidence, limitations and understandable reasons. Backend analysis/review owns authoritative scoring. Missing, null or insufficient evidence must remain explicit: do not convert absence into zero performance, success or failure. Preserve uncertainty through presentation and coaching.

Use calm, specific coaching language that offers options and supports learner autonomy. Do not shame, coerce or promise safety outcomes. Keep active-driving interaction minimal, passive and non-distracting; defer explanations and decisions until the learner can engage safely.

Future richer sources may improve evidence, but require evaluation of consent, provenance, reliability, uncertainty and privacy before supporting stronger claims. A new source does not automatically justify legal conclusions.

Telemetry, scoring and learner-facing claim changes require learning-safety review plus appropriate test review. Active-driving UI changes require distraction review. Inspect evidence-to-claim alignment, explainability, null/insufficient-evidence behavior and coaching language. Document limitations and resolve material findings before acceptance.
